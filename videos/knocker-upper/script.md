## Fact map
- Knocker-uppers worked in Britain and Ireland from the Industrial Revolution, waking workers for early shifts with long poles (often with wire tips), soft hammers or pea shooters. Source: Wikipedia "Knocker-up"; Atlas Obscura "Remembering the Knocker-Ups".
- Mary Smith (London, 1900s–1930s) used a pea shooter and dried peas; famous 1931 photograph by John Topham. Source: Wikipedia "Mary Smith (knocker upper)".
- Pay: "a few pence a week" (Wikipedia); Mental Floss: Mary Smith earned sixpence a week in the 1930s.
- The job faded as alarm clocks became cheap and reliable; a few worked into the 1970s in industrial towns.

## Wording to watch
- "Who woke the knocker-upper? Many worked through the night": a common telling (they were night owls / slept in the day), but the sources found here do not state it directly. Flag as popular explanation rather than documented fact.
- "Jam weker masih mahal dan langka / expensive and rare": sources also say early alarm clocks were unreliable; fine as written.
- Indonesian "beberapa sen" stands for British pennies (pence).
