Your microwave is a piece of war radar. A candy bar gave it away.
In the nineteen-forties, Percy Spencer built radar for the military. He never finished grade school.
One day, he stood beside a humming radar tube. The candy bar in his pocket melted.
So he brought popcorn. It exploded all over the lab. Then an egg. It burst in a coworker's face.
You see, those invisible waves make the water in food shake, like tiny hands rubbing together.
His first oven was as tall as a man and as heavy as a piano.
Today it fits on your counter. And we still use it for popcorn.
